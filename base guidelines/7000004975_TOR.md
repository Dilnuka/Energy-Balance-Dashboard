I. General information
- Brief information on the project
The Partnership for Energy Efficiency in Buildings (PEEB) Cool is a multi-country programme
to transform the buildings sector at scale by supporting resilient and energy efficient buildings
design and operation through financing schemes and policy advisory. It is funded by the Green
Climate Fund (GCF) and co-funded by the German Federal Ministry of Economic Affairs and
Climate Action (BMWK). The programme operates an Investment Facility implemented by the
Agence Française de Développement (AFD) and an Enabling Facility implemented by the GIZ.
The  Investment  Facility  supports  implementation  of  large  scale,  energy-efficient  building
projects  through  concessional  loans,  while  the  Enabling  Facility  supports  the  creation  of
enabling  conditions  and  the  transfer  of  knowledge  required  to  scale  up  energy  efficiency  in
buildings.
PEEB  Cool  is  expected to  be  implemented  in  11  countries:  Albania,  Argentina,  Costa  Rica,
Djibouti, Indonesia, Nigeria, North Macedonia, Mexico, Morocco, Sri Lanka, Tunisia.
## 2. Context
The  National  Energy  Balance  is  a core  national statistical  and  analytical instrument  used  to
track the supply, transformation, and consumption of energy across all sectors of the economy.
In  Sri  Lanka,  the  Energy  Balance  has  traditionally  been  developed  and  maintained  using
spreadsheet-based tools, which, while robust, limit scalability, automation, transparency, and
timely analysis.
Therefore,  the  Sri  Lanka  Sustainable  Energy  Authority  envisages  building  an  interactive
energy data portal to capture building energy, among other energy parameters, with the long
term  view  of  streamlining  the reporting  mechanism to  support  The  Enhanced  Transparency
Framework (ETF) under the Paris Agreement.
This assignment aims to digitize Sri Lanka’s National Energy Balance through the development
of  an  integrated,  web-based  Energy  Balance  Dashboard  under  the  stewardship  of  the  Sri
Lanka Sustainable Energy Authority. The digital platform will transform existing static datasets
into a structured, auditable, and interactive system that supports data submission, validation,
computation, visualization, and reporting.
The digitized Energy Balance Dashboard will enable systematic management of multi-sector
energy  data,  automate  energy  balance  calculations  and  indicators,  and  provide  intuitive
visualizations for policymakers, analysts, and other authorized users.
Overall,  this  activity  will  enhance  the  accuracy,  accessibility,  and  policy  relevance  of  Sri
Lanka’s energy statistics, support evidence-based decision-making, and establish a scalable
digital foundation for future energy planning, monitoring, and reporting.
In this context, GIZ Sri Lanka seeks to hire a consultant to deliver tasks mentioned in part 1. 4
of this ToR.

## 3
The consultant shall undertake the following tasks:

Task 1 - Inception, Requirements Validation, and Work Planning
1.1 Conduct a detailed inception meeting with the Sri Lanka Sustainable Energy Authority
(SLSEA)  and  key  stakeholder  institutions,  including  electricity  utilities,  petroleum  sector
entities, and relevant data custodians.
1.2 Review and validate the functional, analytical, reporting, and institutional requirements
specified in the Energy Balance Dashboard Specifications.
1.3 Undertake a business intelligence and analytics needs assessment to refine dashboard
logic, indicators, data hierarchies, and user journeys beyond the current Excel-based Energy
Balance framework.
1.4 Define and document the following:
- Overall system architecture (frontend, backend, database, analytics engine)
- Data flows, submission workflows, validation logic, and approval processes
- User roles, permissions, and access controls
1.5  Prepare  and  submit  an  Inception  Report,  including  a  detailed  implementation  plan,
milestones, risk assessment, and quality assurance approach.

Task 2 - Data Architecture and UI/UX Design
2.1 Design a scalable and extensible relational database schema aligned with:
- National Energy Balance structure
- ISIC-based sectoral classification
- Enhanced Transparency Framework (ETF) reporting requirements
2.2 Define data standards, naming conventions, metadata structures, and reference tables to
ensure long-term consistency and comparability.
2.3 Design version control, auditability, and traceability mechanisms for all datasets.
2.4 Design wireframes, mock-ups, and interactive prototypes for:
- Dashboards and visualisation pages
- Data submission interfaces
- Administrative and configuration panels
2.5 Apply  usability,  accessibility,  and  responsive  design  principles  to  ensure  compatibility
across devices and browsers.

Task 3 - Development of the Mathematical and Computational Engine
3.1 Develop a modular and configurable computational engine to support:
- Energy balance calculations (supply, transformation, losses, and final consumption)
